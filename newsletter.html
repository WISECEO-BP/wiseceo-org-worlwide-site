    Spotlight: Skilled Trades

    Get paid to learn a skilled trade, with no tuition bill and no college debt.

    Registered apprenticeships let you earn a paycheck while you train for in-demand trades like electrical, welding, and advanced manufacturing. Apprenticeship.gov lists openings you can search by trade and location.

    Who qualifies: Career pivoters and job seekers who want hands-on work, plus local employers who want to train their own talent. Most programs accept ages 18+ (some 16+) with a diploma or GED.

    The path: Search Apprenticeship.gov for your trade and area, contact the program or employer, and apply. Once accepted, you work and train at the same time. Most programs last 1 to 5 years, and many skilled trades take about 4.

    The outcome: A paid position from day one, plus a nationally recognized credential when you finish. Your pay rises as your skills grow. Starting wages vary by trade and employer.

    Your action step today: Go to Apprenticeship.gov, select “Find Apprenticeships,” and search your zip code for one electrical, welding, or manufacturing program. Save the contact info and send your first inquiry.

    TechCred

    Ohio's TechCred program reimburses employers up to $1,000 per credential (up to $30,000 per application) when workers complete approved short-term tech certifications. Ask your employer to apply.

    Integrated English & Career Training

    Learn English While You Train

    Ohio's Aspire program pairs English classes (ESOL) with career training, so adult learners can build workplace English while working toward an industry credential. Look for free classes at community colleges, libraries, or vocational centers in your county.

    Find an Aspire provider →

    Foreign Credential Recognition

    Unlock Your International Degree

    Many skilled immigrants with degrees earned abroad work below their training. Nonprofits like Upwardly Global offer career coaching and guidance on credential review, U.S.-style resumes, and state licensing for work-authorized immigrants, refugees, and asylees.

    Upwardly Global job seeker resources →

    Building Local Enterprise

    Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs) and the Small Business Administration offer micro-loans, mentorship, and technical assistance for small business owners, including immigrant and refugee founders who may lack U.S. credit history.
